# robust_ai_image_detector

Our model aims to detect between Real and AI images. In recent years, there have been a rise in the number of AI generated images on the internet -- some so realistic that humans are no longer able to tell them apart from real images.

We adapted a pretrained ResNet18 backbone by fine-tuning the layers on the SID_Set and CIFAKE dataset

SID_Set: https://huggingface.co/datasets/saberzl/SID_Set/viewer \
CIFAKE: https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/birdy654/cifake-real-and-ai-generated-synthetic-images

## Steps to Reproduce your Results
We first explored with ResNet18 as the base backbone. With the SID_Set and CIFAKE dataset, we trained ResNet18 with default ImageNet weights on different epochs and images per class size. 

We concluded that ResNet18 works best with 12 epochs and 1000 images per class; These variables were then tested on ResNet50 and EfficientNet_B0. While experimenting, it was discovered that ResNet18 on 12 epochs and EfficientNet_B0 on 20 epochs had equally good accuracy rates

We then did further training with images that undergone the transformations:
1. JPEG Compression
2. Gaussian Blur
3. Resize
4. Gaussian Noise
5. Colour Jitter
6. Center Crop

Due to time limit, we only trained ResNet18 on these images. We wrote a script that transforms a portion of our images during training. We further trained our model with 50% transformed and 100% transformed images. The results showed that that the model trained on 50% transformed images had a higher accuracy. As such, this was the model used in our product

## Solution Limitations and Improvements
Our images are trained on a small variety of images only as we only used the two datasets. As such, there might be certain patterns of images generated by other AI models that our model is unable to detect.

Improvements that we could adopt include training out model on a larger dataset with greater variety of photos. Moreover, with more time, we can also train our model to be more robust and experiment with different variables
